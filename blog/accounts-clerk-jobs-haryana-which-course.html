Accounts & Clerk Jobs: Which Course Actually Prepares You

Career Guide Updated 10 August 2026 8 min read

"I want a government or office job — which course should I do?" We hear it every week in Hisar. The honest answer depends on which job. An accounts role and a clerk post look similar from outside, but they reward different skills. Pick the course that matches the job, not the one that sounds biggest. Here's a clear map.

First, know the two paths

Most people asking this question are really aiming at one of two things: an accounts job (junior accountant, Tally operator, GST filing, accounts assistant) or a clerk / office assistant post (data entry, office work, government clerk recruitments). The skills overlap a little, but the core is different — accounts needs bookkeeping and Tally; clerk needs strong all-round computer and office skills.

Don't chase the longest course. Chase the one whose skills the job actually tests.

If you want an accounts job: CDA (or a quick FA top-up)

For accounting roles, Hartron's Course in Digital Accounting (CDA) is the full route. It combines computer basics, accounting principles, and live Tally with GST practice — which is exactly what a junior accountant or Tally operator is hired to do. We break down these roles in detail in our career after CDA guide.

If you already know computers and only need the accounting side quickly, the short Financial Accounting (FA) course is a focused 6-week option covering journals, ledgers, final accounts, bank reconciliation, and GST basics — a solid top-up rather than a full career course.

If you want a clerk / office post: CCA

For clerk-type recruitments and general office roles, Course in Computer Application (CCA) is the fit. It's a broad, government-recognised computer course, and for the Clerk Post it can grant the SETC written-test exemption — one less exam stage. We explain exactly how that works in our CCA + SETC guide. Always confirm the computer-qualification clause in your target notification.

How to choose in three steps

  1. Name the job first — accounts role or clerk post? Be specific about the notification or employer.
  2. Match the skill — accounts needs Tally/GST (CDA, or FA if you only need the accounting part); clerk needs broad computer skills (CCA).
  3. Check eligibility & timing — start early so your certificate is ready before you apply.

Frequently asked questions

Which course is better for a government job — CDA or CCA?

It depends on the job. For accounts roles (Tally operator, junior accountant, GST filing) choose CDA. For clerk and office posts choose CCA, which can also grant the SETC written-test exemption for the Clerk Post. Always check the qualification clause in the official notification.

Do I need CDA if I only want to learn accounting quickly?

Not necessarily. If you already know computers, the short 6-week Financial Accounting (FA) course covers bookkeeping, final accounts, and GST basics. CDA is the fuller route that also includes computer skills and live Tally with GST practice.

What does CDA cost and how long is it at Hartron Hisar?

CDA is 52 weeks and costs Rs 20,000, or Rs 18,000 as a one-time lumpsum.

Can these courses guarantee a job?

No course guarantees a job. They give you government-recognised, job-relevant skills — Tally and GST for accounts roles, or broad computer skills and the SETC exemption for clerk posts — which improve your chances in the process.
