10 Excel Formulas Every Job Seeker Should Know

Practical Skills Updated 27 July 2026 7 min read

Walk into almost any office in Haryana — a shop's back office, a school admin desk, a company's accounts room — and you'll find Excel open on the screen. Knowing a handful of the right formulas is one of the most useful, low-cost job skills you can pick up. Here are the 10 that actually get used, explained in plain language.

Why Excel still matters for jobs

You don't need to be an "Excel expert" to stand out. Most office work uses the same few formulas again and again — adding things up by condition, looking up a value, cleaning text. Get comfortable with those and you can walk into a data-entry, accounts, or admin role and be useful from day one.

Nobody in an office is impressed by fancy formulas. They're impressed by someone who can get the monthly numbers right, quickly, without errors. That's what these ten are for.

The 10 formulas, explained simply

FormulaWhat it does (in plain words)
SUMAdds up a range of numbers — the everyday workhorse.
AVERAGEFinds the average of a range — sales per day, marks, etc.
IFMakes a decision: "if this is true, show A, otherwise show B" (e.g. Pass/Fail).
SUMIFAdds numbers only when a condition is met — total sales for one product.
COUNTIFCounts how many cells meet a condition — how many "Paid" entries.
VLOOKUPLooks up a value in a table — find a price or name from an ID.
XLOOKUPThe newer, easier lookup that replaces VLOOKUP in modern Excel.
CONCAT / &Joins text together — combine first and last name into one cell.
TRIMRemoves extra spaces from messy text — cleans up imported data.
LEFT / RIGHT / MIDPulls out part of a text — the first 4 digits of a code, for example.

If you learn just SUM, IF, SUMIF, COUNTIF and VLOOKUP well, you already cover the majority of everyday office tasks. The rest make you faster and cleaner.

How to actually get good at these

Reading a list won't make you employable — practice will. Here's a sensible way to build the skill:

  1. Type them yourself on real data (a mock sales sheet or marksheet), don't just watch videos.
  2. Break things on purpose — see what error a wrong VLOOKUP gives, so you can fix it later.
  3. Rebuild a real task — recreate a monthly summary from scratch until it feels routine.

Frequently asked questions

Which Excel formulas are most important for a job?

For most office roles, SUM, IF, SUMIF, COUNTIF and VLOOKUP cover the majority of everyday tasks. Add AVERAGE, XLOOKUP, TRIM, CONCAT and LEFT/RIGHT/MID to be faster and cleaner.

Do I need to be good at maths to learn Excel?

No. Basic comfort with numbers is enough. Excel does the calculation for you — your job is to know which formula to use and how to set it up correctly.
