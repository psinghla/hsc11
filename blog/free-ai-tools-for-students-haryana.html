Free AI Tools Every Student in Haryana Can Use

AI & Trends Updated 29 July 2026 7 min read

AI stopped being a buzzword and became a set of everyday tools — and the best ones for a student cost nothing. If you're studying in Hisar or anywhere in Haryana, you can use these free AI tools today to study smarter, write more clearly, and revise faster. Here's an honest guide: what actually helps, what to avoid, and where to go if you want to understand how these tools are built.

Free AI tools worth your time

You don't need a paid subscription to get real value. A few genuinely useful, no-cost options: ChatGPT and Google Gemini for explaining tough topics in simple words and drafting; Microsoft Copilot for quick research with sources; Google Translate for moving between Hindi and English while you learn; and Grammarly's free tier for cleaning up written English. All have free plans that are more than enough for a student.

AI is a study partner, not a substitute for studying. Use it to understand faster — never to hand in work you can't explain yourself.

How to actually use them well

The trick isn't the tool — it's how you ask. Instead of "write my assignment," try "explain this chapter like I'm 15, then give me three questions to test myself." Use AI to break down hard ideas, generate practice questions, summarise long notes, and check your own answers. Ask it to show its reasoning so you learn the why, not just the answer.

Where students go wrong

Two honest warnings. First, AI can be confidently wrong — always cross-check facts, dates, and numbers against your textbook or a trusted source before you rely on them. Second, copying AI output word-for-word teaches you nothing and is easy for teachers to spot. And whatever you paste into these tools can leave your device, so never share passwords, exam papers, or personal ID details. Basic digital safety matters — something we cover in our Fundamentals of Cyber Security (FCS) course.

Frequently asked questions

Are these AI tools really free for students?

Yes. ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Google Translate, and Grammarly all have free plans that are more than enough for everyday study. Free limits and features do change, so check the current terms before exam-time work.

Is it cheating to use AI for studying?

Not if you use it to understand faster and test yourself. It becomes cheating when you submit AI output as your own work without understanding it. Use AI to learn the why, not to skip the learning.

Can I trust everything an AI tool tells me?

No. AI can be confidently wrong. Always cross-check facts, dates, and numbers against your textbook or a trusted source before relying on them, especially for exams.
